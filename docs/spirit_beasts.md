# Spirit beasts

2026-09-17. This document only locks duties and rules. It does not mean they are already implemented. Exploration failure and knowledge layers: [Exploration](exploration.md).

“Produces only after meridian linking, feed is optional, farming must not add attack” remains in force.

## Positioning

The game is an incremental at heart. Spirit beasts are incremental nodes, not a survival sandbox and not daily raising.

The backend may be formulas; the player-facing side must not be only formulas. Rules should be predictable, presentation should have character, and emotionally they can be production partners that represent a trip: after changing a pen's facing they rest somewhere else; they react simply to airflow when a formation starts; they sometimes approach when the player comes home. These may not affect yield.

Exploration spends time, supplies, and risk for a reusable production-line formula. The courtyard connects that formula to qi, terrain, and formations so it keeps running when characters are away.

## Three spaces

| Space | Spirit-beast duty | Explicitly not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outgoing exploration | Wild: observe, lure, kill; patterns go to field notes; seed stock is brought back safely | Long-term farming, idle lines, safe walks |
| Grotto-heaven courtyard | Domestic: after placement, fill a buffer by day; few hard gates + soft preferences | Hunger, escaping to harm people, daily mandatory feeding |
| Yunyin Village | Villager schedules and rumors; buyable basic lines that can run | Hunting grounds, pastures, selling complete habits |

The courtyard stays safe, idle-capable, and jointly buildable online. Exploration stays voluntary risk. Terrain editing: the courtyard owns home clearing and pond shores; exploration only allows small edits that last this trip (ditch, dam, place a formation eye) to verify habits. Do not grow the exploration map into a second grotto-heaven.

## Four steps for one beast

1. **Discover:** go out to observe or kill. Sights and understood patterns go into shared field notes and survive failure. Seed stock, young, or tokens needed to start a line must be brought back safely.
2. **Place:** set a pen, facing, water-adjacency, airflow. Hard gates must be met before daily buffer fill starts, isomorphic with herb fields. Soft preferences change efficiency, qi drain, or adjacency bonuses; they do not decide “whether it can be placed.”
3. **Run:** keep producing offline, online, and when characters leave the courtyard. Stop if qi is short or the buffer is full. No fleeing, starving, or wrecking the house.
4. **Boost:** feed, shapeshift, and adjacency are optional multipliers, not a daily checklist.

Kill and keep are two exchanges of the same object: killing gives one-time materials or recipe fragments; understanding and bringing it back gives a long-term generator.

**Without exploring, one can live, have a basic line, and restock daily gear. Complete habits, unique variants, and understanding that makes a layout actually work remain exploration advantages.** The village sells a simplified machine that works, not the same field notes.

## Shapeshift

Shapeshift is a short-term multiplier, not a third survival state.

- Shapeshift in exploration: used to approach, follow, and trigger behaviors the beast does not open to a “person,” so observation can finish. Cost is qi or short focus. Failure still settles physical loss by exploration rules; patterns already in field notes remain. Not a map-wide persistent transform, and not combat invulnerability.
- Shapeshifted beings in the courtyard: during a window they produce, carry, or guard in place; they disperse when time or qi runs out. Automaton beasts own stable carrying; shapeshift owns bursts; qi paths own long low-cost lines.

## Habits: few hard, many soft

Do not make “three to five rules, all hard gates, find the one correct cell.” The best layout question is “which kind of return do I need more right now.”

- **Very few hard gates:** usually one or two, deciding whether it runs. Example: must be water-adjacent.
- **Several soft preferences:** more qi-efficient by water, helping an herb field but occupying shore, more stable with shelter, facing changing rest schedule. The player trades off; they do not run a legality check.

Observation is “find a clue → form a guess → verify,” not waiting on a progress bar in front of a beast. Example: only stays at a sheltered stream; bait on an open shore does nothing; it will approach only after moving to cover. The courtyard reuses the same pattern.

Do not chase a complete food chain or high-fidelity AI. Content is valued as “one resource or one layout bonus + a set of conditions.”

When the settlement migrates by region, the table of wild formulas that can be met may change; already-placed courtyard lines whose conditions still hold do not stop. Migrating beasts are an opportunity, not a punishment. Do not design “come back on a real-world clock and just miss it.”

## Explicitly not

- Adding hunger, thirst, or harsh survival penalties to the courtyard or village. Failure cost stays on exploration carried items.
- Punishing, losing affinity, or killing stock because the player did not log in to feed.
- Farming or husbandry that directly adds attack. Combat power still goes through realm, arts, and gear.
- Making Yunyin Village a second hunting ground or pasture, or letting traders sell complete habits identical to exploration.
- Stardew Valley–style daily must-feed, must-water as the main loop.
- Making spirit beasts skinned output buttons that only change numbers, not rest schedules and reactions.

## Acceptance

Must hold: after characters leave the courtyard, placed livestock whose conditions are met keep producing by the rules.

Must not hold: the player takes a loss for not logging in to tend them.

Layouts must explain “different placements suit different goals.” There cannot be only one legal answer, and it cannot be only spirit-stones-per-minute.

## Experience slice: one beast first

Validate the loop; do not lay out a bestiary. The experience slice uses one water-adjacent Bamboo Ravine beast:

- **Hard:** must be water-adjacent, or it does not run.
- **Soft:** more stable with shelter (fewer pauses, less qi; does not change whether it can be placed); adjacent herb fields get a small bonus, but the pen occupies shore.
- **Observe:** bait on an open shore does nothing; it approaches only in cover. The experience slice records this by walking to the right-path observation stone, not by shapeshift.
- **Bring home:** patterns in field notes survive failure; the ravine-sprout seed the Bamboo Ravine pen needs must be brought back.
- **Products:** ravine fluff + ravine dew. Fluff is basic padding/textile; the village fluff loom can also make it. Dew is a complete-beast side material, usable by the herb line or window boosts, and cannot be frictionlessly converted to spirit stones.
- **Presentation:** after facing changes, rest position changes; occasionally approaches on return. Does not affect yield.

In the village, a trader can sell a fluff loom that “makes ravine fluff, needs no water, has no field bonus, and produces no dew,” proving basic supply without going out. The experience slice does not build Yunyin Village: this loom sits in the courtyard (a passerby's stall or the master's cabinet) and is usable from the start. The complete stream-side layout still requires going out.

The pen is an entity occupying one living cell; it does not convert that cell into a production cell. Running consumes the spring pool, the same device ledger as the medicine bench, and does not use seepage. Rivers use seepage. Shore is contested by “pen must be water-adjacent” and “fields grow faster water-adjacent.” That is the experience slice's two yards. Do not add a formation set to prove choice exists.
