# Editable terrain workshop

The active demo uses `TerrainData`: a sparse dictionary of land cells, height and optional water elevation. All visible terrain is generated from this data. Missing cells are off-island or holes. The retained legacy gameplay scene has separate historical terrain rules.

Raise/lower move an existing cell by 0.5 units per stroke; height is bounded from -4 to 12. Expand fills absent cells at the selected height. Contract removes cells, water and automatic decoration. Drag strokes interpolate their path and change each visited cell once. Undo/redo retain the latest 64 strokes.

Snapping aligns the brush center to the picked cell center, including the owning cell on cliff boundaries. With snapping off, circular coverage follows the cursor continuously; underlying terrain still uses cells and 0.5 height increments. The construction grid is optional and does not define ground materials.

Water painting selects one of exactly eight absolute elevations: 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 or 3.5. Only lower floors accept water; raising the floor through its water surface removes that water. The data retains 47 normalized eight-neighbor states, with diagonal normalization. The active rendering joins whole equal-elevation regions into shared smoothed contours rather than displaying individual cell polygons. Terrain contours support the selected water elevation above neighboring ground, including deep floors. A bounded terrain-derived texture supplies shallow bank and floor-depth color. It publishes with the completed immutable terrain snapshot, so staging cannot update visible water ahead of its mesh. This is editable pond geometry and shading, not fluid simulation. A contour outlet facing lower water or absent land generates an outward spill apron, curved waterfall ribbon and landing foam. Shared world-height flow profiles keep neighboring ribbons joined even when receiving terrain is lowered or removed. Falling water uses moving polygonal veins. Landing foam has joined raised rings with smooth normals and an opacity fade; clipped chunk triangles retain those gradients and normals. The sky is a generated cloud-only backdrop mapped to the fixed camera view, with neutral cubemap lighting. It contains no terrain or props; editable scene objects remain 3D geometry.

`ContourTerrain` builds elevation outlines from authoritative data. World-anchored sampling keeps remote outlines stable during local edits. Hole-aware cap decomposition preserves openings even when a triangulation would surround a small hole. Tapered, irregular cliff vertices form a connected rock volume; inset is limited by local thickness so narrow necks and isolated cells do not invert. 8×8 chunks partition grass, foam, cliff, water and waterfall surfaces. Cliffs use fixed vertical samples and local profiles, keeping distant rock shapes stable when an edit adds an elevation. Broad contour spans receive lateral subdivisions at no more than .65 units; narrow spans retain their existing profile. A world-anchored clipped-octahedron distance field produces stronger physical rock shoulders and recesses, fading on narrow base profiles to keep opposing walls separated. Geometry and material share the 2.2×1.8×2.2 site dimensions and distance metric. Exposed wet walls and immediate mouth-side neighbors limit protruding rock shoulders behind the stream envelope. Clearance fades at the crest to preserve cap joins. The reference mesh regression samples curtain triangle centers against actual cliff triangles; arbitrary layouts and unsampled intersections remain unproven. Shared relief values cache by exact warped sample position and clear each refresh. Broad profiles have reduced directional/vertical wave noise. A cellular material supplies stronger color and normal facets across the connected body. Grass caps subtract higher regions so hidden grass cannot protrude through tapered rock. Shared cap triangulation can span remote chunks, so each revision regenerates the active owning chunks. A spatial triangle index limits work within each chunk. Rebuilds are bounded to two chunks per frame; a complete immutable terrain snapshot, its decorations, supported props and picking data publish together. Continuous strokes finish the in-flight snapshot and coalesce newer changes into the next one. Idle frames do not rebuild terrain. Picking intersects the actual rendered triangles, with nearest-cell ownership resolution for exposed facets that protrude beyond a cell boundary.

Existing CC0 Kenney model parts use chunk-local MultiMeshes. Broad solid tree canopies assemble 17 retained oak leaf parts above the taller, broader detailed-tree wood mesh. Seven unequal core masses overlap ten smaller rim clusters, with intended dimensions normalized from source bounds, local three-axis tilt and height-varied tint. This breaks up the previous flat repeated tiers while keeping original forks. Four transformed wood extremal vertices were found inside the current retained crown meshes; this is finite contact evidence rather than exhaustive branch coverage. Original low forks receive a runtime upward lift into the raised crown, preserving source topology and ground-contact vertices. Runtime leaf meshes receive one subdivision and a partial irregular ellipsoid blend (.48 for tree crowns, .68 for bushes/vines), preserving more angular source shape. Only surface-referenced vertices determine leaf bounds; the current imported oak already has 114/114 vertices referenced, so this guards other inputs rather than correcting its current bounds. Imported originals are preserved. Model parts are merged by kind before instancing. Coordinate/seed hashing places independent kinds deterministically. Surface grass follows ground height; surface rocks partly embed; occasional cliff rocks follow the tapered exposed profile, with width and depth reduced on thin shells. Runtime shoreline stones use flattened stone_largeD parts with deterministic width, height and angle variation; short grass and subtle ground color facets reduce spiky repetition. Hanging foliage assembles flattened leaf sprays. The supporting cliff mesh uses shared area-weighted normals, interpolated across chunk cuts. Its world-anchored rock shader divides each site by three oblique planes, softens vertical curvature shading and narrows whole-rock contrast. The latest broad-span refinement changes physical geometry as well as shading. Normal quantization was discarded after review because it created stretched patches. Embedded rocks retain physical flat facets and use a matching shared palette selected from each transformed instance origin, avoiding the body shader’s nearest-site search on every rock pixel. The retained generated sky backdrop receives runtime mipmaps and five filtered LOD 2.5 samples, with reduced cloud contrast and paler cyan. Original bitmap/import metadata are preserved. The visible-sky branch changes independently of the neutral cubemap lighting; sun and ambient settings stay unchanged. Submerged cells suppress surface vegetation and rocks. Original sources are unmodified. Runtime materials adapt the pack to the scene palette. Falling water has shallow physical folds with lateral subdivisions and fixed .5 world-height samples; terminal interpolation preserves mixed-height joins. Crests and aprons follow each column, and smooth waterfall normals survive chunk clipping. A dedicated translucent material blends XY/ZY world projections with vertical streaks and quieter polygon veins; both patterns move downward. It does not write depth or cast solid shadows. Pond material remains opaque with the previous shallow-bank/depth math. Transparent overlaps and all outlet orientations are not exhaustively verified. Pond edging, path stones, fences, reeds, lilies and hanging foliage derive support from the same surface query. The cottage, bench and lantern hide when dense footprint samples find absent, wet or uneven support. The retained cottage uses material-name-based workshop shading: plaster receives warm panels through the gables; corrected timber and stone avoid the roof branch. Panel axes use object-space normals, preserving post placement across view changes. Source model geometry and materials are unchanged. Bench and lantern are placeholders.

Layout JSON under `user://terrain_editor/layout.json` preserves terrain and seed separately from legacy gameplay saves. Loading validates the complete candidate before replacement, rejecting corrupt data, nonfinite values, coordinates beyond ±1024, more than 65536 cells, duplicate cells and invalid water levels.
