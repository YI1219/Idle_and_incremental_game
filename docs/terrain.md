# Editable terrain workshop

The active scene is `scenes/terrain_editor.tscn`. Its 2026-10-06 look-development candidate is **READY FOR ART REVIEW**; user aesthetic approval remains separate. [Current runtime views, motion, checks and performance](../art/review/terrain_fidelity/2026-10-06/review.md) supersede earlier round status. [Historical geometry/vegetation evidence](../art/review/terrain_fidelity/2026-10-05/review.md) is not a new verification.

The workshop uses `TerrainData`, a sparse 2D XZ dictionary of cells with height and optional water elevation. It is not a 3D voxel volume. Missing cells mean holes or off-island space. All visible land, automatic detail, supported props and picking share this authoritative data. The retained legacy gameplay scene has separate historical terrain rules.

Raise/lower move existing cells by 0.5 units per stroke, bounded from -4 to 12. Expand fills absent cells at the selected height; contract removes cells, water and automatic detail. Drag strokes interpolate their path and change each visited cell once. Undo/redo retain 64 strokes. Optional snapping aligns brush centers to picked cells, including cliff-boundary ownership. With snapping off (the default), circular coverage follows the cursor continuously; heights remain in 0.5-unit steps. The optional construction grid does not define the surface material.

Product camera orientation is fixed. Keyboard and middle drag pan horizontally without changing height. Only the wheel changes height/zoom; recenter/reset/presets preserve height. The look-development harness manually rotates the camera for inspection without enabling gameplay orbit. See [camera behavior](camera.md).

Water painting offers exactly eight absolute elevations: 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 and 3.5. Only lower floors accept water; raising the floor through its water surface removes water. There are 47 normalized eight-neighbor data states with diagonal normalization. Whole equal-elevation regions render with shared smoothed contours, not individual cell polygons. This is editable pond geometry and shading, not fluid simulation.

A bounded terrain-derived texture carries bank distance in R, floor depth in G, receiving-impact strength in B and wet coverage in A. A lower wet cell adjacent to a higher wet cell seeds disturbance; a short breadth-first traversal fades it only through connected receiving water of the same elevation. Dry cliffs, distant/disconnected lakes and upper source water stay calm. Linear queue cursors avoid repeated removal/search costs. The texture publishes with the completed immutable terrain snapshot, keeping water shading synchronized with mesh edits.

The lake material is opaque and nonmetallic. Continuous world-space wave derivatives drive normals, converted to view space for the shader. Mild color modulation uses the same waves; it is not scene reflection. Terrain bank/depth data controls a subdued color transition. Godot's dielectric BRDF supplies angle-dependent response, with roughness .16 plus localized disturbance .06 and specular .30. Directional static sky radiance contributes to water response; cottage/tree geometry is not captured. No SSR, dynamic probe or refraction is added. Default beds are uniformly shallow, so depth response is subtle; broad regular wave bands remain a visual gap.

An outlet facing lower water or absent land generates an outward apron, curved waterfall ribbon and receiving foam. Shared world-height profiles join neighboring ribbons across mixed receiver heights. Connected runs encode across-stream position, drop distance and reciprocal width in vertex colors; chunk clipping preserves those interpolated values. Fully exposed closed rings bypass side fade. The separate waterfall shader uses continuous elongated streaks advecting downward in world XY/ZY projections, bounded uneven side taper and no global emission. It does not write depth or cast solid shadows. Existing folds, outlet geometry and foam stay intact: crest strips fade upstream, and low unequal elliptical landing swells fade at their perimeter. Outer falls receive no invented terminal splash. Straight crests, striped sheets and soft landing foam remain fidelity gaps.

`ContourTerrain` generates hole-aware grass caps and continuous tapered cliffs. World-anchored samples keep remote shapes stable during local edits. Contour thickness constrains narrow necks, isolated cells and hole walls. Matching spans merge through elevation intervals; canonical endpoint/column samples maintain joins. Broad cliffs retain staggered flat geometric normals and physical rock relief; narrow profiles retain constrained smooth shells. Wet-mouth clearance keeps protrusions behind stream envelopes. The procedural bottom remains -4.5, compatible with the minimum terrain height -4. Geometry is unchanged in the current material pass; elongated wedges, sharp local corners and the smoother contracted base remain limitations. Restrained gray variation now reduces face-color contrast without replacing real normals.

8×8 chunks partition grass, cliff, water, waterfall and foam surfaces. Shared cap triangulation may span remote chunks, so each revision regenerates affected owning chunks. A spatial triangle index bounds chunk work. Complete immutable terrain, decoration, supported props and picking snapshots publish together at two chunks per frame. Continuous strokes finish an in-flight snapshot and coalesce later changes into the next one. Idle frames do not rebuild. Picking intersects actual rendered triangles, resolving owning cells on protruding cliff facets.

Existing Kenney CC0 model parts and retained Quaternius curved/wispy grass use chunk-local MultiMeshes. Parts merge by kind; the Quaternius single surfaces preserve UVs and their `Grass.png` palette. Original assets remain intact. Trees retain ten compact groups assembled from thirteen oak leaf parts; shrubs retain six unequal leaf parts; hanging foliage fits actual cliff triangles. Surface stones partly embed, while the connected procedural body supplies cliff rock volume. No separate automatic cliff-rock layer or locked decorative ground is added. Grass and supported nature follow regenerated terrain. The ground shader has broad restrained color variation and quieter flecks.

Shoreline stones retain seeded size/orientation variation. A world-space gap field opens supported gently curved bank stretches; sharper bends and drops retain anchors. Occasional reeds require existing dry/equal-height support. This introduces grassy stretches rather than a complete stone ring; it does not add a physical shallow shelf. Stone anchors are checked, not entire enlarged footprints. Submerged cells suppress land vegetation/rocks. Cottage, bench and lantern hide when their dense footprint samples find absent, wet or uneven support. The refined cottage retains material-role shading and original geometry. Bench and lantern are placeholders. Bulky similar crowns, sparse wiry grass and some shrub/path overlap remain art gaps.

Lighting remains the baseline daylight setup: directional energy 1.10/angular size 3.5°, ambient .42 and SSAO radius .85/intensity .95/power 1.2, with linear tone mapping and 4x MSAA. The generated cloud-only backdrop remains screen-mapped for the fixed gameplay view. The cubemap branch uses a directional cloud gradient for static sky radiance. It contains no ground or props. Arbitrary orbit is not a product feature; unrestricted angular wrap of the bitmap is not globally verified.

Layout JSON under `user://terrain_editor/layout.json` preserves terrain/seed separately from legacy gameplay saves. Loading validates the whole candidate before replacement, rejecting corrupt/nonfinite data, coordinates outside ±1024, more than 65536 cells, duplicates and invalid water levels.

Fresh checks in [verification.json](../art/review/terrain_fidelity/2026-10-06/verification.json) cover impact, shoreline, waterfalls, contours, data, render publication, decoration, incremental updates and runtime/camera/save behavior. They are finite fixtures, not proof of arbitrary topology, every prop footprint, complete temporal quality or target-device performance. Older detailed geometry tests remain available but their historical outputs are not current evidence.

Reproduce the scene and evidence:

```sh
godot --path . scenes/terrain_editor.tscn
python3 tests/run_preview.py tests/capture_terrain_lookdev.gd --resolution 1672x940 --script-arg final
godot --path . --resolution 1280x720 --fixed-fps 60 --write-movie /tmp/terrain_motion/frame.png --script tests/capture_terrain_lookdev.gd -- final motion
python3 tests/run_preview.py tests/benchmark_terrain_fidelity.gd --resolution 1280x720 --interactive --script-arg lookdev --script-arg offscreen --script-arg final_isolated
python3 tests/run_preview.py tests/terrain_water_impact_test.gd --headless
python3 tests/run_preview.py tests/terrain_editor_runtime_test.gd --headless
```

Create `/tmp/terrain_motion` before the movie command. Run benchmarks alone, sequentially; submission intervals with the minimized window are not presented FPS. Metal GPU timing is unavailable here. Thin-laptop support, larger maps and long-idle stability need separate measurement.
