# Editable terrain workshop

The active demo uses `TerrainData`: a sparse dictionary of land cells, height and optional water elevation. All visible terrain is generated from this data. Missing cells are off-island or holes. The retained legacy gameplay scene has separate historical terrain rules.

Raise/lower move an existing cell by 0.5 units per stroke; height is bounded from -4 to 12. Expand fills absent cells at the selected height. Contract removes cells, water and automatic decoration. Drag strokes interpolate their path and change each visited cell once. Undo/redo retain the latest 64 strokes.

Snapping aligns the brush center to the picked cell center, including the owning cell on cliff boundaries. With snapping off, circular coverage follows the cursor continuously; underlying terrain still uses cells and 0.5 height increments. The construction grid is optional and does not define ground materials.

Water painting selects one of exactly eight absolute elevations: 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 or 3.5. Only lower floors accept water; raising the floor through its water surface removes that water. The data retains 47 normalized eight-neighbor states, with diagonal normalization. The active rendering joins whole equal-elevation regions into shared smoothed contours rather than displaying individual cell polygons. Terrain contours support the selected water elevation above neighboring ground, including deep floors. A bounded terrain-derived texture supplies shallow bank and floor-depth color. It publishes with the completed immutable terrain snapshot, so staging cannot update visible water ahead of its mesh. This is editable pond geometry and shading, not fluid simulation. A contour outlet facing lower water or absent land generates an outward spill apron, curved waterfall ribbon and landing foam. Shared world-height flow profiles keep neighboring ribbons joined even when receiving terrain is lowered or removed. Falling water combines moving vertical strands and quieter polygonal veins. Connected spill runs share normalized across-stream/world-drop fields and reciprocal width in vertex colors, with barycentric interpolation through chunk publication. Fully exposed closed rings bypass side fade. The shader adds brighter cyan/white flow and bounded irregular side taper, preserving a center on narrow streams; extreme widths retain color-precision limits. Landing foam has unequal higher peaks within the previous footprint and joined raised rings with smooth normals and an opacity fade; clipped chunk triangles retain those gradients and normals. The sky is a generated cloud-only backdrop mapped to the fixed camera view, with neutral cubemap lighting. It contains no terrain or props; editable scene objects remain 3D geometry.
