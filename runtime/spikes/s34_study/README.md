# Studies S3 and S4: look and island generation

Throwaway code. One tool that builds an island from cells with a new generator and draws it with a new look, so both can be judged on pictures against the frozen Godot project and on a few numbers. Its checkpoint G1 is `art/lookdev/20261122_g1/` (`README.md`, `compare.html`); the owner rejected its look B on 2026-11-23, and the tool now draws the natural look of the Godot baseline; the package for the owner's review is `art/lookdev/20261123_natural/` (`README.md`, `compare.html`). The owner reviewed it on 2026-11-23 and asked for four adjustments (a hollow terrace joint, buildable slopes and ramps, rock walls after the Godot baseline, clearer water); rounds r8 to r10 made them, and the second review set is `art/lookdev/20261123_natural/final2/`. On 2026-11-25 the owner asked for two more things: rounder, softer rock-wall edges (round k1) and a short appear-and-settle motion after edits (round k2); their review set is `art/lookdev/20261125_settle/` (`README.md`, `compare.html`).

Nothing here is the start of the real crates. What carries forward is the design, the measurements and the list of defects.

## Run it

```
cd runtime && cargo build --release -p s34_study
cd .. && tools/perf/gpu_run.sh 600 runtime/spikes/s34_study/tools/shot.sh OUT_DIR \
    --layout=runtime/data/reference/layout_7301_0.json --seed=7301 --views=whole,game \
    --tag=NAME --metrics=OUT_DIR/metrics.json > OUT_DIR.log 2>&1
```

Run from the repository root (data paths are relative to it). `shot.sh` writes the frames (3024x1964) to `OUT_DIR` and reduced copies (1300 px wide) to `OUT_DIR/small/`; with `CROP="Y X"` set it also writes a 1300x850 crop at native pixels. Send `gpu_run.sh` output to a file; do not pipe it.

The whole review set (stills of the five islands with and without ramps, dusk and night, three islands, the moving-camera stills, the edit test on five islands, then the benches with the machine's load recorded beside them) is `art/lookdev/20261123_natural/checks/render_review.sh [OUT]`, run detached with `nohup`. A run that finished leaves `OUT/<name>.ok` and is skipped next time, so the script can be restarted after an interruption; `OUT/DONE` is written at the end and `OUT/FAILED` lists runs that did not finish. `OUT` defaults to `final2/`.

## Slopes and ramps (rounds r9 and r10)

- Layout version 2: a cell may carry `"slope": [gx, gz]`, the rise in metres per metre toward +x and +z (gradient length at most 0.6, about 31 degrees; no water). Its `height` is the surface height at the cell's centre, a free real number; the surface over the cell is that plane. Cells without `slope` are flat; version-1 files load unchanged. Water levels keep the export's encoding (half metres, -1 for none).
- The ramp stroke `ramp(a, b, width)` (`field::ramp`): `a` and `b` on land; refused when either end is water or off the island, the length is under 2 m, the gradient `|hb - ha| / length` exceeds 0.6, the two ends stand at the same height, or the strip would cover water. The ends' heights are those of the cells under the drawn points. The stroke is then snapped (`field::snap_stroke`, round r10): a stroke within 7.5 degrees of a grid axis is made exactly axis-aligned, its centre line on a cell edge for an even width and on a row of cell centres for an odd one, its ends on the nearest cell edge along the axis, so the ramp is exactly `width` cells across and meets the flat lawn exactly at both ends; any other stroke is kept as drawn. Every cell whose centre lies within `width / 2` of the snapped segment (inclusive) and between its ends becomes a slope cell with `height = ha + (hb - ha) t` and `slope = (hb - ha) / length * direction`. A diagonal ramp's ends and sides follow staircases of cell edges; its plane stands off the flat terrace there by up to about 0.7 m times the gradient, which the 0.25 m rule below absorbs or turns into a small soft bank.
- Steps with slopes: each side of a cell edge is its cell's plane. Two neighbours (slope or flat, neither holding water) whose surfaces agree along the shared edge within 0.25 m at both ends (`CONTINUOUS`, raised from 0.08 m in round r10) are continuous: no step; the crease is rounded over 0.4 m either side (`CREASE`, `Field::cell_ground`). Otherwise the edge is a step whose rise varies along it; each half edge is labelled by which side is higher at its middle, so a step whose sides swap over along an edge is handled. A chain of steps beside a slope cell is a wall as a whole when its rise reaches 0.75 m anywhere: a ramp's cheek is one wedge of cut rock that continues the terrace wall it cuts and tapers to nothing where the rise runs out (its two sides close on each other over the last 0.7 m, `TIP_FADE`); under that it is the soft bank. Chains of flat cells keep the per-piece rule, so islands without slopes build exactly as before (and take the flat-only code path).
- The wall ribbon (`field::ribbon`) takes its two grounds per end, so its height follows the varying rise; the roll over the brow drops at most half the rise; where the sides swap within a piece it is split at the crossing.
- Lawn: the rosette grass on a slope cell grows as on flat ground (no lip thinning), each root turned to the slope. The ground painting follows the plane.
- Props: heights above Godot's own cell come from the scene's layout, so an edited layout does not move them. On a slope a tree, bamboo, bush, bench, lantern or the scholar's rock stays upright and sinks its base to the lowest ground under its footprint; stones, path stones, flowers and tufts tilt with the ground. The synthetic scatter puts no tree within 1.2 m of a slope cell.
- Rim: the rim plan, the lip and the rim skirt read the field's height, so where a ramp reaches the outline the skirt closes the sloped lip down to the blocks.
- Junctions (F1): where three or more steps meet at a cell corner with a wall among them, the grid cells within 0.45 m of the corner are cut into the sectors between the steps' first pieces; each sector takes the ground of the bounding step nearer to the point (a wall's side, a pond's held rim, or a soft step's blend), and every wall ray's ribbon runs to the corner itself without overlap or roll, so the walls share the corner's vertical edge and turn it as one piece of stone. The slim posts of round r8 are gone.
- Demonstration layouts in `runtime/data/reference/`: `layout_7301_0_ramps.json` (three ramps joining the four lawn levels), `layout_4242_1_slope.json` (a broad hillside), `layout_2104_0_ramps.json` (diagonal ramps); the ramp views of `--views` show them.
- Locality (round r10 review set, `final2/edit_test.log`): on the four layouts and the 4,096-cell island, the `ramp` and `diag` strokes changed the top surface at most 4.0 m from the stroke and the rim plan at most 4.04 m; nothing in any of the 25 strokes changed beyond 12 m.
- Not here: an interactive ramp tool (phase 3, with the edit pipeline), a free-form hill brush, paths over slopes (phase 2).

## Appear and settle (round k2)

The owner's request of 2026-11-25: a short Tiny Glade-like appear-and-settle when terrain or items are edited, not a growth simulation. `src/settle.rs` and the `settle_*` functions in `src/shader.wgsl`.

- When an edit lands, the island is rebuilt once (as before) and a small settle map is made over the edit's dirty rectangle only (the cells it touched plus 4 m; 0.25 m texels, two vec4 each: the old ground height, the new height, the vertical band that may move, a weight, how much of it is new land, a start delay, and how much grass regrows), plus one record per prop instance (origin and start delay, or none). Both go to two storage buffers in the globals group; one clock value in the light set (slot 46, `L_SETTLE`) drives the motion. The frames of the motion need no mesh work at all; with the clock off (below 0 or past 1.5 s) every `settle_*` function returns its input.
- Ground (vertex stages of the island, its shadow, grass roots and props' origins): where the height changed, a point within the band round the old and new heights is pressed to the old height and released over 0.55 s with an ease-out that overshoots about 6 % of the change (`settle_back`, c1 = 1.3): a raised patch grows out of the old lawn as a walled plinth, a lowered one sinks, a ramp leans out of the wall it cuts. The weight is the change grown by 0.5 m and softened over another 0.5 m. New land (cells the edit added, and 1 m past the new outline into what was not land) is lifted whole, rim blocks included, from 0.6 m below; its weight stops at its own texels, so old land beside it does not bend. A start delay of 0.045 s a metre from the stroke's centre makes it ripple outward.
- Shading while the ground deforms: the fragment stage turns the authored normal by the rotation that takes the undeformed facet's normal to the deformed one's (`settle_normal`), so a lawn pressed into the old wall's step shades as a step, and a lifted plate keeps its smooth shading.
- Grass on changed ground (and in a ring about half a metre wide round each placed item's clearing) regrows: height from nothing with an overshoot of about 10 %, width from 35 %, 0.42 s, starting 0.10 s after the ground under it plus up to 0.14 s of per-root jitter.
- Placed items grow about their origin: height over 0.5 s with about 12 % overshoot, width 0.05 s behind it with about 7 %; items start 0.09 s apart in the order placed, plus the ripple. Every prop rides the ground under its origin. `props::load_with` bakes placed items (a copy of one of the scene's examples of that kind) after the scene's own; `PropVertex.mat.zw` carries the instance id the vertex stage reads the record with.
- Latency on layout 7301: in round k2, 68 to 87 ms rebuild, 0.2 to 0.4 ms for the settle map, 17 to 28 ms upload. Round k5 builds the island on all cores (`field::par_map`: the field's blurs and grids, the top surface's sample cache and rows, the grass lattice in bands of rows, joined in order so the result is identical; top surface, body and props at once; the props' scene export parsed once and kept): the rebuild is 24 to 29 ms against 63.5 ms on one core (`S34_SERIAL=1`) on the same busy machine. In the window, edit to first motion frame on the glass is 122 to 140 ms (see "Real-time window"). The rebuild is still whole-island, with every stage local by construction; rebuilding only the touched chunks remains the edit pipeline's next step.
- Round k5, removal: a `Transition` (settle.rs) compares the island before and after an edit. Old geometry that leaves is kept for the motion as ghosts and drawn after the island's own (ranges in light slot 48): land an edit removed, with the rim that hung off it, lifts a hair and falls away into the cloud with an ease-in (0.7 s); items removed by hand, or gone with their land, swell a little and shrink to nothing (0.45 s); drained water sinks to its bed (0.6 s); grass on removed land falls and withers. Land left standing at a cut dips 18 cm and settles back. Water that rises or appears comes up from its bed and settles. Ghosts are hidden once their motion is over or with the clock off. Lowering is the compressed motion as before, so a lowered patch or a lowered plinth sinks and settles.
- Round k5 fixes: a lawn surface pulled into a steep face during the motion (the old wall at the start of a ramp) is drawn earth-coloured for that moment; the wall between new land and a lower old terrace moves with the new land (no slivers); edge settle and water are covered.
- Known limits: the shadow map has to be redrawn every frame while anything moves (0.2 to 0.7 ms at 3600 x 2338).

## Brush tools and the construction demonstration (round k5)

`src/brush.rs` is the edit path every edit takes: a stroke (tool, the pointer's path over the island, radius and the tool's settings) applied to the cells and items, after the plan's commands (2.3) and the frozen editor's `apply_brush` rules: a round brush applied at steps a third of its radius apart along the path, each cell changed at most once per stroke; raise and lower by 0.5 m (heights -4 to 12 m); expand adds cells at the stroke's build height (the ground under its first point, in 0.5 m steps; at most 4,096 cells); contract (erase) removes cells; water sets a level on the cells under it that lie lower (the spring model of plan 3.8 is not built, so this is the frozen editor's water brush); dry clears water; ramp is `field::ramp`; place puts an item (a copy of one of the scene's examples of its kind) at each point; remove takes away the items whose origin lies within the radius (recorded by their source key, so they stay removed across rebuilds; `props::Items`).

`src/demo.rs` (`--demo=FILE`) builds an island from one standalone mound by a script of strokes, each through `brush::apply`, rebuilt and played with the settle or removal motion from the moment it is released, 30 frames a second of scene time; the brush outline on the ground is printed per frame for the recording. Not exercised: the mouse and the picking of the point under it (the paths are given in island coordinates), and the jade preview while dragging. The script `data/demo_mound_build.json` uses every tool: expand (four strokes), raise (a soft bank, then a wall, a small hill), lower (a hollow, then a walled pit; a basin at the rim), water (a pond in the pit; a pond at the rim that spills as an outer fall), ramp, contract, place (trees, bushes, stones, flowers, a lantern), remove, dry and water again, and lower (the small hill away).

## Rock massing (round k5)

Owner request of 2026-11-26: the island's rock read as upright polygons stacked together. `body.rs`, keeping round k1's rounded edges:

- Rim blocks lean: the foot stands 0.15 to 1.0 m further in than the top, from a field over the world (inward from the island's mid-scale outline, amount and a sideways drift of up to 0.3 m from smooth noise about 6 m across), taken at the two ends of the front, so two neighbours meeting at a junction lean alike there and no gap opens; the middle of the front bows up to 0.2 m; the foot is cut more out of level along the rim (up to 14 cm a metre).
- A face wider than 2.6 m is folded once (wider than 5.5 m twice), 8 to 22 cm in or out, so a broad face is a few planes of one rock.
- Buttress slabs vary more (2.8 to 6 m wide, 3.4 to 6.2 m tall), turn 11 to 27 degrees off the wall (never square to it: a slab parallel to the wall read as a framed door), lean out 0.35 to 0.9 m at the foot and roll up to 0.7 m sideways, have a folded front, and one in two has a smaller companion propped against its side, set lower and turned the other way; slightly more faces offer one.
- Round k5b, stripes: junctions with a step under 45 cm, or at a concave turn of the rim, share one sharp corner (the round k5 corner arcs of 3.5 cm, the 0.22 m overlaps and the backing plates behind every joint showed as thin vertical stripes; the plates are gone); folds only on faces over 4 m (twice over 7 m), 6 to 14 cm; no face narrower than 1.4 m (merged into a neighbour).
- Repairs: the rim skirt (where the lawn's lip stands above a block's top) stands flush with the block's front with the lawn carried over it, rolled and fringed (it was a band at the outline over a short stone shelf); junctions with a step under 25 cm keep square corners, and every junction has a backing plate of lit stone 2.5 cm behind it following both leans (a groove, a pipe-like slot or a hairline crack into the island read as defects); a narrow face under 1.6 m wide stands nearly flush instead of recessing 0.27 to 0.55 m; a block's depth stops at the island's edge (on a narrow strip of land it reached across and its cap showed as a grey shelf on the far side).

## Edits stay local (round k5b)

Owner review of 2026-11-26: unloading blocks changed other blocks, and the island seemed to re-render. `settle::diff_solid` (`S34_DIFF=1`) compares every triangle before and after an edit. Height edits were already local; edits that change the outline (erase, expand) changed the body up to 22 m away, for four reasons in the generator, all fixed:

- The hanging columns and the liner were on grids anchored to the field's corner, which moves with the island's bounds, and were sized from the 7 m blur. Both grids are now anchored to the world, and their depth comes from `body::edge_distance` (the distance to the outline found by probing rings out to 8 m).
- The rim blocks leaned along the 2.5 m blur's gradient; now along the outline's own normal.
- The grass planted along the rim took its random numbers from a counter running over every rim edge of the island, so adding or removing one edge changed every lip plant after it; each plant now takes them from its own place (the edge's midpoint and its index along the edge).
- The rim plan's breaks come from windows 2 to 6 m wide either side, so a changed outline re-planned faces about 10 m away. An edit passes the old island's breaks and its rectangle (`body::Prior`): breaks outside the rectangle grown by 2.5 m that still lie on the outline are kept, and only those inside are planned again, so every face wholly outside is the same face as before.

Erase now changes nothing beyond 9.0 m (rim blocks within 7.1 m; was 20.9 and 10.1 m), expand within 8.0 m (was 22.2 m). Height edits and items are unchanged in this respect (only the edit's own triangles). Because the rebuilt island differs from the old one only near the edit, the whole-island swap no longer shows as a restart.

Unloading (any edit that lowers the ground) retracts (round k5b): the old volume above the new ground (a raised block's top and walls and its grass, or the lawn over a new pit) is a ghost that sinks by the height lost (texel channel `c.w`, the most any texel within 0.75 m fell) with the ground's ease and overshoot, and is hidden once it meets the new ground; the new ground stays where it is, items there ride down, grass on the lowered cells regrows. Before, the new surface was pressed up to the old height and released, so it spanned from the old top to the ground as stretched faces and then flattened. Sequence `unload`: a block raised 1 m lowered to the lawn in one edit (`Edit::Brushes`).

## How an island is built

Cells (1 m, heights in 0.5 m steps, an optional water level, and in a version-2 layout an optional slope) are the only input. Every derived value depends only on cells within a few metres, which is what keeps an edit local (`--edit-test`: no rim face and no top-surface vertex changes beyond 12 m on the four layouts and the 4,096-cell island, ramp strokes included). Rule since round r8: no pixel may read as a hole into the island; `--debug=parts` over every review camera checks it.

- `src/field.rs`
  - Fields on a 0.25 m grid: a blurred occupancy whose 0.5 contour is the island's outline (sigma 0.45 m), two wider blurs for how deep inside the rim a point is, a blurred water mask for banks.
  - Terrace steps: every cell edge between two land cells of different heights is a step. The steps form a graph on the cell corners; its chains (between junctions of three or more steps and the rim) are subdivided at 0.5 m, relaxed (20 passes) and held within 0.6 m of the cell edges, so a stair of cells becomes one straight or gently curved run. Each piece keeps the two levels of the cell edge it came from, so a 2 m drop stays one wall. Pieces sit in a 1 m bucket index.
  - Height: from which side of the nearest relaxed step a point lies on. A rise under 0.75 m is a soft lawn slope (0.44 m wide); 0.75 m or more is a wall, a hard step. A step in a pond bed is soft. A pond is held where a wall or the outline would spill it: a narrow bank 0.10 m above its surface, except in a fall's notch, where a sill lies just under the surface.
  - Top surface: one mesh over the grid, clipped at the outline. A grid cell crossed by a wall is cut along the relaxed contour, each part at its own level, and the cut is filled by a ribbon: the upper lawn rolls 3.5 cm over the edge, then cut rock goes straight down (in the bed's colour under a pond's surface). Behind each piece of wall a backing face (5 cm behind the cut) shows rock through the slits where pieces meet; it stops at a corner instead of reaching past it, so it is never seen through a three-step corner. A piece of surface that bridges two levels faces the way it tilts; a flat piece is lawn even where the field's slope is steep. Lip plants are set along the outline and along the walls' brows, and grass is planted along the walls' feet. Each place where a wall, a bank or a ramp meets the outline is closed. Lower lawn beside a held pond is no longer painted as that pond's bed (it had taken the upper pond's level: the dark cavity of the hollow joint the owner saw).
- `src/body.rs`: the rim plan (outline points anchored to a world lattice, breaks chosen within their own window, long faces split and paired) and two bodies: `Hull`, a low-polygon inverted terrain, and `Karst`, that hull as a hidden liner with rock hung on it after the Godot cliff (round r8, sizes read from the Godot cliff scripts): one tall vertical block per rim face, running straight down the whole visible height with no taper and no bedding step, its foot 7.6 to 9.4 m under the cap in the root mist, every vertical edge rounded (round k1: a real arc of three pieces with smooth normals, 0.12 to 0.22 m, a stone cap's top edge rolled over 0.14 m); a few broad buttress slabs (Godot's shoulder groups, about one every 8 to 12 m of rim, kept by a one-hop world-anchored rule) standing a metre or two proud with a wedge top falling outward and along their length; and a second tier of fewer, larger hanging columns. Blocks reach back only as far as the ground over them stays at their top, and every block is capped; a block standing forward of the outline carries a lawn cap that rolls over its edge with a grass fringe along its open edges (round r10), not a bare stone ledge. Where the rim steps down (a terrace wall or a pond reaching the outline), a rock skirt at the outline closes the gap between the higher lip and the block top; the liner's rim takes the lowest ground within 0.7 of its grid so it never stands above a lower lawn.
- `src/water.rs`: pond surfaces from the water cells and the cells round them, clipped where a wall or the outline holds the pond. Each junction (pond edges spilling into lower water or off the island) pours through one notch, a sheet `min(junction width, 1.6 m)` wide centred on it and placed on the relaxed wall or the outline. The sheet starts on the pond just behind the lip and rolls over it, falls on an arc, and lands in a soft foam patch with a low veil; an outer fall frays into the cloud sea.
- `src/props.rs`: the frozen Godot scene's props from `runtime/data/reference/scene_<seed>_<preset>/` (exported by `tools/export/export_scene_props.gd`), baked into static world-space vertices and drawn by ports of three Godot shaders (`cliff_rock`, `decor_vertex_color`, `foliage_cards`).
  - On an exported layout each instance is refitted to this tool's ground (its height above its Godot cell kept; lilies and reeds keep their height above the water). Instances off the island are dropped and counted. Round r9 (F2): a stone (garden, cliff and shore stones, path stones) whose footprint is over a pond and whose underside stands clear of the bed is lowered onto the bed, partly under water, or moved to the nearest dry shore point within 2.5 m when the water is deeper than 60% of its height (`PROPS ... stones set on a pond bed, ... moved to the shore`). A prop whose footprint (the part within 0.4 m of its base) touches a terrace wall or a fall's notch is moved to the nearest clear lawn or shore point within 1 m, or dropped (`PROPS ... moved off walls`).
  - On a synthetic island the scene's trees, bushes, bamboo, garden and shore stones, flowers, tufts, reeds and lilies are scattered by seed at the source layout's count per square metre of dry land (or of water for the shore and pond kinds). A tree's trunk and crown, two objects in the export, stay one instance. Trees stand in loose groups off water, walls and the rim, bushes gather round them, shore stones line the ponds, lilies take open water. Garden stones come in clusters of two to five of mixed sizes, started mostly on pond banks, at the feet of terrace walls and beside trees, now and then on open lawn. The scholar's rock, bench, lantern and the stepping-stone path are not scattered.
  - Props keep the grass clear round them. In the terms pass they are drawn per 8 m chunk, culled to the view, nearest first.
- `src/decor.rs`: procedural stand-ins (pines, broad-leaved trees, rocks, stepping stones, bamboo, lily pads) for `--props=none`.
- `src/grass.rs`: the frozen project's rosette grass, instanced: one clump mesh per detail level and one 32-byte record per root, 28 roots a square metre, colour relative to the ground, levels per 8 m chunk from the projected leaf width.

## How a frame is drawn

`src/render.rs` and `src/shader.wgsl` (wgpu 30; see `runtime/README.md`):

1. Shadow map (4096, 16-tap soft lookups; 6 taps for grass and leaf cards), cached while the sun does not move.
2. Terms pass: island, props, grass write a display albedo, sun visibility, baked occlusion and a normal. Round r9 (F4): the cut-out leaf cards first write depth only (atlas cut-out, a vertex shader with only position and atlas coordinate), then the island, props and grass draw, then the leaf cards are shaded with an equal depth test and no discard, so one fragment per pixel runs the leaf shading. Props are culled in 4 m chunks. Shadow taps turn by a fixed rotation (one cos and sin a pixel); leaf cards facing away from the sun skip their taps where the back-lit term is negligible. Island stone is painted here (gouache wash, brush planes, dry strokes, mossy brow under the lawn lip, after Godot's `stone_surface` and `stone_cliff_character`).
3. Occlusion at half the internal resolution with a depth-aware blur; far taps read a view-distance pyramid.
4. Resolve in linear light: sun, sky and ground hemisphere, fill; the far field; near air. Stone takes the reference's strong difference between sun and shade (round r8); a terrace wall or a ramp's cheek in shade is lit at least to a minimum fill (round r10), so a low wall under a grass overhang reads as stone, not as a dark slot. The far field is ported from the frozen project: its painted sky, six cloud decks and three rings of distant mountains placed as Godot placed them (`data/mountain_poses.json`). The near air (fog and far haze) is scaled by the camera's distance to its orbit centre over the review set's whole view, so a larger island seen whole is not washed out. Below the island the rock dissolves into what lies behind it along the same ray (round r9, F3): the sky and cloud decks drawn at a quarter of the output resolution in a pass of their own and read bilinearly, so the root fades into whatever cloud is behind it from any side and at any hour; the level and softness of the dissolve are unchanged. (The water pass, which has no far-field textures, keeps the fixed mist colour for falls that drop into it.)
5. Water pass, premultiplied into its own target: ponds as clear water over a visible bed (round r8, after Godot's `water.gdshader` refraction: the pale pond bed with pebbles seen through the surface, refracted; absorption per metre of view path per colour channel, red first, so depth turns jade; a clearer shore margin; light lines on the bed; Godot's ripples; the screen-space reflection laid lightly on top), falls (Godot's waterfall threads and ropes, a per-hour tint calibrated to the reference), spray and foam.
6. Down pass: the water composited, the internal samples averaged in linear, Godot's filmic curve, one sRGB encode, contrast and saturation, dither. Then FXAA if asked.

Colours are authored as display values and decoded to linear in the shader; backdrop colours are pre-inverted through the tone curve so they appear as written. The hour's palette shifts come from the frozen project's blended style presets (`tools/blend_preset.py`).

## Real-time window (round k5)

`--window` (`src/window.rs`, `src/gputime.rs`) puts the picture on the Mac's screen in real time and measures what reaches the glass. Run from the repository root:

```
runtime/spikes/s34_study/tools/window_run.sh OUT TAG [--seconds=20] [--scenarios=idle,moving,editing] [options]
```

The script stops if the Mac is not on AC power, records the load (uptime, top five processes) before and after, and runs `s34_study --window` under `gpu_run.sh`; the window closes by itself (Escape also stops it). Outputs: `OUT/TAG_<scenario>.csv` (one row per presented frame: interval, acquire wait, encode, submit, present call, upload, GPU span, interval on the glass, state-sampled-to-glass latency, shadow, settle clock), `OUT/TAG_<scenario>_passes.csv` (each pass's own GPU time), `OUT/TAG_summary.txt` and `.json`.

- Window: a borderless window covering the built-in display (`--fullscreen=cover`, 3600 x 2338 drawable at the owner's scaled 1800 x 1169 mode, 120 Hz ProMotion; NSScreen reports 120 frames a second). `--fullscreen=native` is macOS full screen, which keeps the notch band free: 3600 x 2252.
- Picture: the existing renderer, layout 7301, camera `game`, day, the moving-camera mode (`--scale=1`, FXAA). The last pass writes straight into the surface texture (`--present=direct`, plain `Bgra8Unorm` tagged sRGB, since the renderer writes display values itself) or into the final texture that a pass then copies (`--present=blit`). Offscreen stills are bit-identical to 3bc9d74.
- Scenarios: `idle` (camera still, shadow map cached), `moving` (orbit at 0.72 rad/s), `editing` (every 2.5 s the settle edit `--edit=raise` or its undo; the rebuild runs on a worker thread through `rebuild_island`, the one function to swap for an incremental update; the worker also builds the settle map and the GPU buffers, `Renderer::prepare`; the frame loop only swaps them in, `Renderer::install`, and starts the settle clock; the shadow map is redrawn while the motion runs).
- Measured: CPU times per frame (CACurrentMediaTime); GPU time from timestamp queries at pass boundaries (Metal offers TIMESTAMP_QUERY only, not inside passes or encoders; a frame's queries are resolved two frames later, because a resolve in the same command buffer can run before the last pass ends); and the time each frame reached the glass, `MTLDrawable.presentedTime`, read in a presented handler that a wrapper of `-[CAMetalLayer nextDrawable]` attaches to every drawable (`--presented-time=off` to leave it out). With Fifo the CPU-side present cadence (a proxy under back-pressure) agreed with the glass cadence within 0.1 fps on every run; with Immediate it did not (85.7 against 85.1, frames dropped). Glass intervals are exact multiples of 8.33 ms.
- Options: `--latency=1|2` (`desired_maximum_frame_latency`; default 1), `--present-mode=fifo|immediate` (Metal offers no Mailbox), `--latch=late|early` (sample the frame's state after or before the drawable wait; default late), `--upload=worker|main`, `--worker-qos=interactive|default`, `--pace=HZ`, `--shadow=always`, and `--render-scale=F`, a disclosed quality reduction (the picture drawn at F of the surface size and stretched), off by default.

Findings (2026-11-26, Mac busy throughout: load 7 to 9, a VPN process at 100% CPU):

- The frame costs 10.5 to 12.5 ms of GPU time at 3600 x 2338, so 120 Hz is out of reach. Own share per pass (moving, p50): terms 4.1 to 4.8, resolve 3.4 to 3.8, water 0.6 to 0.8, ssao 0.6 to 0.7, fxaa 0.5, down 0.5, far low 0.35, blur 0.42, ao depth 0.07, shadow 0.7 to 0.9 (only during a settle motion). Offscreen at the same size: everything 13.0 ms, without grass 12.4, without leaf cards 12.6, without SSR 12.5, without SSAO 10.8: the terms pass is the island and props shading, not the grass.
- With two frames in flight (the naive loop) Fifo presents 80 to 93 frames a second, every interval 8.33 or 16.67 ms (constant judder), 48 ms from sampled state to glass, and installing an edit's buffers on the frame loop stalls it 13 to 56 ms.
- Default now: one frame in flight, late latching, direct present, upload prepared on the worker: a locked 59.5 to 59.8 frames a second with even 16.67 ms intervals, 31 to 32 ms from sampled state to glass, edits installed in 0.3 to 0.8 ms. Cost: with one frame in flight a frame whose GPU time passes about 14 ms takes 25 ms (seen while the settle motion redraws the shadow map on a loaded machine).
- Edit to first motion frame on the glass: 140 to 180 ms either way; the rebuild (70 to 120 ms) dominates, and it runs 20 to 30 ms slower when the frame loop sleeps in the drawable wait (user-interactive QoS on the worker helps only marginally).
- `--render-scale=0.8` (a quality reduction) brings the GPU to about 8.5 ms, still not enough for a steady 120 Hz, so it is not worth its softening (game view against native 3600 x 2338, bilinear stretch: mean difference 1.4/255, PSNR 37.2 dB, 3.2 % of pixels off by more than 8/255, mostly edges and grass); `--pace=60` with two frames in flight paces worse than one frame in flight.

## Known limits

- Where three steps meet next to a fall's notch, the corner's top can show a small angled step in the lip. Island stone seen from behind is shaded as if it faced the camera.
- Rock: at native pixels the stone brushwork is blotchier and less vertically streaked than Godot's. Round k1 rounded the rock's edges (`body.rs`: `rounded_ring`, `tall_block`'s roll and crowned slab top, `rim_junction` fillers; `field.rs`: `SoftCorner` and the wall's rolled top row): a buttress slab's edges are 0.18 to 0.30 m arcs with a 3 to 6 cm crowned top shaded by its own normals (no crease); where a rim block's end stands out past its neighbour, a concave fillet fills the V and both corners' shading turns only as far as the step is deep; the columns under the island have 0.15 to 0.22 m arcs of two pieces. Round k5 repaired the shelf where a rim skirt met a forward block and the thin joint where two blocks met nearly flush (see "Rock massing"). Still visible: where new land leaves a one-cell notch against a lower terrace (the expand sequence), a small steep sliver of lawn at the notch's inner corner.
- Ramps are one plane per cell (a ramp is a straight incline; there is no hill brush). The ramp's own triangles cost nothing measurable in a frame, but an island with slopes builds its top surface 10 to 20% slower (slope-aware lookups near slopes, more junctions).
- Props are moved off wall faces by their footprint only; a tree's crown may still overhang a wall, as in Godot.
- The three-island wallpaper view (`--scene=trio`) still uses the older mound far field and cloud sea, because the ported decks vanish toward the horizon at its low camera.
- The 4,096-cell island at `--scale=1 --fxaa` with scattered real props runs close to the 12 ms budget (10.8 to 11.2 ms idle in round r9; the r10 review set was benched on a busy machine and reads 13.4 to 14.0 ms, see `final2/load_at_benches.txt`); grass (about 2.1 ms in `game`), screen-space occlusion (1.7 ms) and the cut-out leaf cards (1.4 ms) are most of the cost. The leaf pre-pass and the r9 culling and shadow-tap changes saved about 0.3 ms; culling grass in 4 m parts drew fewer blades but was 1.5 ms slower (more instanced draws) and was left out.
- The tool renders off screen only; frame times are wall-clock over 120 frames with the shadow map cached.

## Findings worth keeping

- Authoring colours as display values and then writing them through an sRGB target made every frame about 0.09 brighter and half as saturated. The pipeline must be one or the other throughout.
- Spacing outline points by arc length makes the whole rim plan slide when the outline changes anywhere. Points have to be anchored to the world, lattice lines must not coincide with cell edges, and ties have to be broken by something that does not depend on order.
- Blurring raw heights, or blurred level sets, gives terrace edges that follow the cell stairs on shallow diagonals. Relaxing the step chains themselves (with every level that steps at one cell edge kept on one piece) and cutting the surface along them gives straight runs and true walls.
- A material family interpolated across a triangle passes through values that match no family; snap it per pixel.
- Soft noise used as brushwork reads as dirt. Thin, hard-edged strokes in sparse clusters read as drawing.
- Blocks that pass through each other at shallow angles leave thin slivers. A real construction needs blocks that are cut against each other or kept apart.
- Screen-space occlusion was the largest single cost of a frame until it ran at half the internal resolution with a depth-aware blur.
- On a large island the cut-out leaf cards of the exported trees are the largest cost: they are drawn over each other many times. Opaque crown masses or distance levels are needed in the real renderer.
- Holes into an island come from surfaces whose edges are interpolated between different levels (a liner rim, a block top at the lowest ground of its face) and from open block tops. A hole-finding view (`--debug=parts`) over every review camera finds them faster than reading frames.
