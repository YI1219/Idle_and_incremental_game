Partly yes. Of the three problems, the coupled changes came from the island generator itself, and the other two from round k5's own edit motion and junction pieces. Each was measured and fixed in this round.

  1. Coupled changes: the generator was not local for edits that change the island's outline. A new check compares every triangle before and after an edit (S34_DIFF=1). Edits that only change height (raise, lower, ramp, water) were already local: only top-surface triangles inside the edit changed. Erasing or adding land at the rim changed rock up to 21 to 22 m away (erase: 1,010 body triangles more than 3 m from the stroke, 686 more than 6 m). Four causes in the generator:
    • The hanging columns under the island and the hidden liner were placed on a grid anchored to the corner of the island's bounding box. An edit that moved the bounds moved the grid, and with it every column. Their depth also came from a 7 m blur of the island that reaches about 20 m.
    • The rim plan chooses where one rock face ends and the next begins by letting each point compete with its neighbours 2 to 6 m either side, then splits and pairs long faces. A changed outline could therefore re-plan faces about 10 m away, and every block built on them changed.
    • The rim blocks' lean read a 2.5 m blur.
    • The grass planted along the rim took its random numbers from one counter running over every rim edge of the island, so adding or removing one edge changed every lip plant after it, all round the island (visible as a thin line of change along the whole rim). It now takes them from each plant's own place.
    Fixed: world-anchored grids sized from the distance to the outline within 8 m; leans from the outline's own normal; lip plants keyed by their own place; and an edit keeps the rim plan's face boundaries outside the zone it changes, so faces wholly outside are the same faces. Erase now changes nothing beyond 9.0 m (rim blocks within 7.1 m, was 10.1 m); expand within 8.0 m (was 22.2 m). What still changes beyond 3 m is the face or two next to the edit and the hanging columns under the island within 8 m.
  2. The render restart came from how edits were applied. Every edit rebuilds the whole island and swaps all of it in at once. Whatever the generator had changed away from the edit therefore appeared in one frame, with no motion over it. The swap itself costs the frame loop nothing (it is prepared on a worker since round k5). With a local generator it now changes nothing outside the edit's zone, so the whole-island rebuild no longer shows as a restart. A per-chunk incremental rebuild is no longer needed for correctness; it remains an option for latency on large islands.
  3. The strange polygons when unloading a raised block were not the generator. They came from round k5's settle motion, which raised the new, lower surface to the old height and released it. With the block's walls gone, that surface spanned from the old top to the ground, then flattened. Now the old block retracts: it sinks into the lawn by the height it loses and merges. The vertical stripes were round k5's junction pieces (3.5 cm corner arcs, 0.22 m overlaps, backing plates), also not the original generator; removed.
