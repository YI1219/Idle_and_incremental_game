# Floating-island terrain revision

Status: functional review passed; visual acceptance pending. The goal remains active.

The active workshop now uses shared rounded contours over sparse 2D height data, rather than a 3D voxel volume. Fixed vertical cliff samples and local profiles preserve distant rocks after local edits. Exposed grass caps subtract higher regions, including holes. An immutable revision builds two chunks per frame, then publishes its meshes, instanced detail, support props and picking together. Newer input coalesces into the next snapshot, so sustained painting continues to publish terrain.

These captures drain ordinary incremental updates and do not force a full rebuild to mask stale geometry. Each edit starts from the composition layout. [Capture metadata](captures.json) records brush centers, radii and changed cells. PNGs remain local per repository policy; the capture script reproduces them.

The wider upper terrace, shifted ponds and falls, supported trees, cottage, fences, bench and lantern now follow the reference composition more closely. Existing CC0 Kenney source models and the refined cottage remain unchanged. The supporting cliff body now carries stronger world-anchored material facets and rounded cellular shoulders, with occasional embedded stone_largeA models rather than repeated vertical stacks. The geometry/material site field uses matching 2.2×1.8×2.2 dimensions; relief caches by exact warped position within each refresh. Extra shoulders fade out on narrow profiles, retaining the existing one-cell-strip shape. Broad profiles have reduced directional and vertical wave noise. The latest actual cliff close-up breaks up long diagonal folds into more distinct rock regions, though broad smooth bulges and contrasting patches remain a modest improvement rather than a reproduction. Runtime canopies use 14 overlapping lobes with broader, lower wood proportions, a partial irregular ellipsoid blend and per-lobe tint. The final thickness adjustment reduces the flattened appearance. Hanging foliage assembles flattened leaf sprays; shoreline stones use flatter stone_largeD parts with deterministic size and angle variation. Shorter grass and subtle lawn color facets calm the ground. The cottage has broader workshop proportions and adjusted timber shading. Bench and lantern remain placeholders. No custom model-generation service or purchase was used. The built-in image-generation tool produced a cloud-only sky backdrop; its [source record and exact prompt](../../../source/skies/2026-10-05/cloud_backdrop_v01.json) accompany the runtime copy in `assets/terrain_editor/cloud_backdrop_v01.png`. The backdrop fills the fixed camera view and uses neutral cubemap lighting; terrain and props remain interactive 3D geometry.

Pond shallow-bank and floor-depth color derive from the same terrain snapshot. Its texture publishes alongside the completed mesh, keeping partial rebuilds from changing visible water early. This adds a shoreline appearance transition, not refraction or fluid simulation. Falling water uses finer moving polygonal veins with reduced emission. Joined raised foam rings have interpolated smooth normals, a transparent edge and slight surface motion; chunk clipping preserves their opacity and normals. Independent review found no important remaining issue after the downward animation correction.

Visual differences remain: broad cliff areas are smoother than the target's broken rock volumes; tree crowns still show assembled rounded lobes, open lawns remain simpler, and shoreline stones repeat; falls still read as sheets with patterned veins and a modest splash; the cottage lacks finer timber bays and ornament; the detailed cloud backdrop contrasts with the simpler tree and cliff surfaces. This is a closer editable reconstruction, not an accepted reproduction. Fine custom-model changes remain subject to the user-operated asset workflow.

New splash tests failed 24/26 before raised/faded foam, and 26/27 before opacity interpolation survived chunk clipping. Final checks pass 27/27. Review also caught upward vein animation; world-height advection now uses a positive time offset for downward movement. The new bounds regression failed 4/9 before restricting its AABB to surface-referenced vertices and now passes 9/9. A real Godot importer probe found current tree_oak leaf geometry has 114 vertices, all referenced, and equal full/active bounds; this protects other inputs rather than explaining the current asset appearance. Independent final review found no Important correctness issue in the foliage adjustment. The unchanged 28-script gameplay suite belongs to an earlier revision. [Verification](verification.json).

Measured on Apple M4 Pro, Metal Forward+, 1280×720, shadows and SSAO enabled, vsync disabled: three 12-second runs after two-second warmups. Median frame time 5.504–6.042 ms; p95 14.584–16.013 ms; worst sampled frame 82.158 ms. Four edits per run became visible in 119.897–161.139 ms. Every frame is sampled independently through normal two-chunk update waits. [Measurements](performance.json). Default terrain has 595 cells; samples end at 593 after edits. Draw calls include shadow passes (496). Static memory was about 57.9 MiB; reported video memory was 206.9 MiB, including the new cloud texture. Capture runs use fixed-step timing and screenshot UI counters are not benchmark evidence. Uncached cellular relief initially produced edits of 159.820–203.872 ms and worst frames of 99.899 ms; the exact-position cache reduces repeated site searches, bringing this sample nearer the previous revision’s 114.557–152.343 ms edit range. It remains a measured cost, and run-to-run variation precludes a broad performance improvement claim.

These short development-machine samples do not establish large-layout scaling, target laptop performance, or long-idle power/stability. Function, visual quality and performance acceptance remain separate.
